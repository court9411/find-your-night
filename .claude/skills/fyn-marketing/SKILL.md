# Find Your Night — Marketing Skill

## Who We Are
Find Your Night (FYN) is an AI-powered nightlife discovery app for Cincinnati — built to help anyone find the right vibe for their night without guessing. Not a Yelp clone. Not a travel app. The recommendation from someone who actually knows.

The app is for everyone. The launch strategy starts with Black Cincinnati — that's where the founder has real relationships, real credibility, and real content. Community-first launch, universal product vision.

**Built by**: Courtney (solo founder)
**Current focus city**: Cincinnati, OH
**Soft launch moment**: Cincinnati Pride weekend, June 26–28 — good timing, not the identity

---

## Brand Voice

### The persona
Write like the most plugged-in person in the city — knows the promoters, knows which nights are actually worth going to, knows where the real vibe is. The friend whose recommendation you follow without asking questions, regardless of who you are. Confident. Real. No corporate filter. The insider tip that makes anyone feel like they're in on something.

### What the voice is NOT
- Not "inclusive" in the watered-down corporate sense
- Not trying to appeal to everyone — that's how you appeal to no one
- Not over-explaining or selling too hard
- Not performative — no empty affirmations or buzzword diversity language

### Tone by context
| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Event promos | Energetic, direct, vibe-forward — make them feel like they're missing out |
| Venue spotlights | Insider, specific, like a recommendation from someone who actually goes |
| App features | Confident, benefit-first — what does this do for *you tonight* |
| Founder/BTS content | Real, scrappy, no polish — people root for authentic builders |
| Launch content | Historic energy — this is something new for Cincinnati |

### Words and phrases that fit
"The move", "you already know", "find your night", "the vibe", "tonight", "for us", "locked in", "the scene", "what's really good", "pull up"

### Words to avoid
"Thrilled to announce", "excited to share", "inclusive", "for everyone", "diverse" — any language that sounds like a press release or a corporate DEI statement. Also avoid whitewashing the voice to sound neutral.

## Target Audience

**Launch community**: Black people in Cincinnati, 21–38, active in the nightlife/social scene — this is where trust and early content come from
**Broad audience**: Anyone in Cincinnati who wants a real recommendation before they walk in the door, regardless of background
**Partners**: Black promoters and venue owners — they're users AND distribution. Their trust is the content pipeline.

**The positioning**: The app is universally useful — finding your vibe for the night is something everyone wants. The launch community gives it credibility and character. A recommendation from someone plugged into Black Cincinnati nightlife is useful to *everyone* who wants to actually have a good night.

**What users want** (universal): To know where to go tonight without scrolling through reviews written by people who don't get the vibe. To trust the recommendation before they show up.

**What early adopters want specifically**: To find spots where they'll feel at home. To have an app that gets their scene — not one that treats their nightlife like a tourism category.

## On the Pride Launch
Pride weekend is a smart soft launch moment — big crowd, high energy, lots of people looking for things to do. But FYN's identity is not a Pride app. Don't lead with Pride in the brand. Lead with the app. Let the launch timing speak for itself.

Content during Pride weekend can acknowledge the moment without making it the whole story:
- "Cincinnati's out this weekend — we're here for all of it"
- "Whatever your night looks like this weekend, Find Your Night has it"
- Not: "Happy Pride from Find Your Night 🏳️‍🌈" as a primary identity post

## Founder vs. App Account — Keep These Separate

**App account** posts about: venues, events, vibes, features, the city. The product speaks for itself.

**Personal page** is for: reaching out to promoters and venue owners so they know you're a real person, not a bot. That's relationship building, not content strategy — keep it there.

The only time founder presence belongs on the app account is a launch post ("I built this, here's why" — once) or a significant milestone. It's not a recurring content type. Making the app's social about you gives off "I want attention" energy when it should give off "I know where to go" energy.

## Content Pillars (what the feed actually is)

1. **Venue spotlights** — specific, vibe-forward, makes people want to go
2. **Event announcements** — what's happening, when, what to expect
3. **App features** — when something new ships, one post, benefit-first
4. **City content** — "best [x] in Cincinnati right now" — builds authority without being self-promotional

That's it. The feed is about the city and what's in it.

## Copy Rules
1. Lead with the vibe, not the address or the hours
2. Be specific — "cocktail bar where the regulars know each other" beats "great atmosphere"
3. One idea per post. Don't cram.
4. CTA is always direct: "Find it on Find Your Night" not "check it out"
5. Read it out loud — if it sounds like a brand, rewrite it to sound like a person
6. Write for someone deciding where to go *right now*, not someone casually browsing
7. Don't sanitize the voice to sound neutral — neutral doesn't build community
